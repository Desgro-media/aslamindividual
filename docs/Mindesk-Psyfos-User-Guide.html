Part 1Start here

1.1 What is this software?

Mindesk (also called Psyfos) is the program your clinic uses to run the day. It keeps everything in one place:

  • Bookings — who is coming, to which therapist, at what time.
  • Money — what a session costs, what was paid, what is still due.
  • Client records — each client’s history, notes after every session, and how their case is going.
  • Follow-ups — reminders that a client should come back, so nobody is forgotten.
  • Reports — a monthly picture of the whole clinic, and one for each therapist.

You open it in a web browser (Chrome or Edge works best), on a computer, tablet or phone. You do not install anything.

1.2 Who uses it?

Every person has their own login. What you see on the screen depends on your role.

TherapistSees today’s sessions, runs the session, writes notes, updates the client’s status, sets the next follow-up.Schedule, My Clients, Case History, Session Notes, Follow-up, My Session Reports

1.3 How to log in

  1. Open the clinic’s website address in your browser and go to the Login page.
  2. Type your email and password (the clinic owner gives these to you).
  3. Click Sign In. You land on your own home screen: therapists see Schedule; everyone else sees Overview.
Keep your password private

Never share it, even with a colleague. If someone needs access, the owner can create a login for them in Staff. If you forget your password, ask the clinic owner to reset it (see Part 8).

1.4 Finding your way around

Every screen has the same frame, so once you learn it, you know all of it.

  • Menu is shorter than you expect? That is normal. Each person only sees what they are allowed to use.
  • Log out with the small arrow icon at the bottom of the menu, especially on shared computers.
  • Coloured chips next to a client’s name show their case status (see Part 2).

The idea in one sentence

The front desk books and collects money; the therapist treats, writes notes and says when the client should return; the front desk then books that return visit.

2.2 The five session statuses

Every client always has exactly one status. It shows how their case is going. The therapist sets it when completing a session, and can change it at any time from the client’s profile. Every change is saved in a history, so you can always see who changed it and when.

StatusUse it when…What happens next
New CaseThe client is new and treatment has only just started.Usually changes to Ongoing after a few sessions.
OngoingRegular sessions are in progress.Stays until the client improves or leaves.
Periodic Follow-upRegular treatment is finished, but the client comes for occasional check-ins (for example monthly).A follow-up date keeps appearing.
TerminatedTreatment ended properly, by agreement.Case is closed. Waiting follow-ups are cancelled automatically.
DroppedThe client stopped coming without finishing.Case is closed. Waiting follow-ups are cancelled automatically.
Changing your mind is fine

If a Terminated or Dropped client comes back, just set the status again to New Case or Ongoing and set a new follow-up. Nothing is lost.

2.3 Follow-ups explained

A follow-up is a promise to bring a client back. It is created by the therapist (usually when completing a session) and handled by the front desk.

Pendingdate set, no time yet
→
Bookedfront desk gave a time
→
Donethe session took place

A follow-up can also become Cancelled (for example when a case is closed or the client no longer needs it).

  • A client can have only one open follow-up at a time. This stops double reminders.
  • If the due date passes and nobody booked it, it turns Overdue (shown in red) so the front desk sees it first.
  • If the therapist already chose a time, the follow-up is booked straight away and goes into the calendar.

Part 3 · For receptionistsReceptionist guide

Your job: make sure every client is booked, paid, and brought back when the therapist says so.

3.1 Overview — your starting screen

The Overview shows how the clinic is doing right now. You do not have to do anything here; it is a quick look.

3.2 Appointments — every booking in one list

TabMeaning
PendingA client asked for a time, and the clinic has not accepted it yet.
Awaiting PaymentA payment link was sent. The booking is held until the client pays.
Verifying PaymentThe client says they paid (sent a screenshot). A staff member must check it.
ConfirmedThe booking is firm. The therapist will see it on the Schedule.
CompletedThe session has taken place and the therapist finished it.
CancelledThe session will not happen.

3.3 New Booking — the 5-step wizard

Use this whenever a client calls, walks in, or messages you. Click the blue New Booking button on the Appointments page (or in Patients, or on a lead). A window opens and walks you through five steps. A green tick shows each finished step.

Step 1 — Client

  1. Type the client’s phone number. The software looks for an existing client.
  2. If the client is found, their details fill in. Use that record — do not create a second one.
  3. If not found, type their name (email is optional). Click Continue.

Step 2 — Service

Choose what the client needs. Services are grouped as Counselling, Therapy, Assessment, Career, Other. Tap one, then Continue.

Step 3 — Therapist

Pick the therapist. Only therapists who offer the chosen service are listed. A receptionist must always choose a therapist — you do not have your own calendar.

Step 4 — Date & time

  1. Choose In-person or Online.
  2. Check the session length (it fills in from the service; change it only if the therapist asks).
  3. Pick the date. The free times for that therapist appear as buttons.
  4. Click a time (it turns blue), then Continue.

Step 5 — Payment

Check the summary card (client, service, therapist, when, mode, fee). Then choose how money will be handled:

ChoiceWhat happens
Collect at the front deskSession is confirmed now. The amount due appears in Pending Payments for you to collect later.
Client is paying nowYou record the money received (amount and method) as part of the booking.
Send the client a payment linkBooking is held as “Awaiting Payment” until the client pays online.
Time saver

On the Patients page, the New Booking button already knows the client. On the Leads page, Book session starts the wizard for that lead.

3.4 Pending Payments — collecting the money

This is your “money to collect” list. It holds every confirmed session where money is still due.

  1. Find the client and click Collect Payment.
  2. The amount already shows the full balance. For a part-payment, type a smaller number — the rest stays in the list.
  3. Choose the method: Cash, Card, UPI, Bank Transfer or Insurance.
  4. Add a short remark if useful (for example “first instalment”). Click Confirm.
Be careful

Always pick the true method (Cash vs UPI). The owner uses this to match the day’s cash with the money in the drawer and the bank.

3.5 Follow-ups — bringing clients back

When a therapist says “see this client again around 15 October”, it lands here. Your job is to give it a real time.

  1. Open the Needs scheduling tab. Start with the red Overdue rows.
  2. Call or message the client to agree a time.
  3. Click Book session, pick the date, mode and time, and choose how payment is handled.
  4. Click Book session again. The follow-up moves to the Booked tab and a normal appointment is created.

Need to add a reminder yourself? Use Add follow-up at the top right.

3.6 Leads, Patients, Billing and Monthly Report

Leads

A lead is someone who showed interest but has not booked. Call them, then click Book session to turn them into a client with a booking.

Patients

All the clinic’s clients with their status chip. Filter by status, search by name, open a profile, or start a booking.

Billing

Every invoice, with filters ALL / UNPAID / Partial / PAID / WAIVED and a month picker. Mark Paid records a full payment.

Discounts and waiving

Front-desk logins cannot waive or discount a fee. If a client needs a concession, ask the clinic owner.

Monthly Report

One page that tells the whole story of a month. Change the month with the arrows. Use CSV to download for Excel, or Print to make a PDF.

Part 4 · For therapistsTherapist guide

Your job: see the client, write the notes, say how the case is going, and say when the client should return. Everything else is handled for you.

Your menu has six sections: Schedule, My Clients, Case History, Session Notes, Follow-up, My Session Reports.

4.1 Schedule — your day

Before the session

Click Case history on the client’s card to read earlier notes in 30 seconds. The chip beside the name (for example Ongoing, Session 5, Returning) tells you the client’s stage at a glance.

4.2 Complete a session — notes, status, follow-up in one go

After the session, click Complete session. One window handles three things so nothing is forgotten.

  1. Session notes (optional): fill the four SOAP boxes — Subjective, Objective, Assessment, Plan. You can skip this now and write them later in Session Notes.
  2. Session status: tap one of New Case Ongoing Periodic Follow-up Terminated Dropped. The current status is already selected.
  3. Next follow-up: keep Schedule one ticked, choose a date, optionally a time, and add a note for the front desk. Choose “Decide later” for the time if the client will confirm later.
  4. Read the blue summary line at the bottom, then click Complete session.
All or nothing

The window does everything together: marks the session complete, saves the notes, changes the status, and sets the follow-up. If something fails, nothing is half-saved. Just try again.

Choosing Terminated or Dropped

These close the case. The follow-up part is hidden and any waiting follow-up is cancelled. If you chose by mistake, change the status again from the client’s profile.

4.3 My Clients and the client profile

My Clients lists your clients. Filter by status, search by name, and click one to open their profile.

Scroll down on the left to find the Session status card. It shows the current status, the next follow-up and the status history. Click Update to change the status.

If you schedule a session yourself

When you click Schedule on a profile you can set the session length and leave the payment for the front desk. It then appears in their Pending Payments list.

4.4 Case History, Session Notes and Follow-up

Case History

One page to review a whole case. Pick a client on the left; on the right see their details, a summary, and a timeline that mixes sessions, status changes and follow-ups in date order.

Session Notes

Your note to-do list. Finished but not completed shows sessions whose time has passed but are not yet completed — click Complete session. Notes to write lists completed sessions without notes — click Write notes.

Follow-up

The same follow-up list the front desk uses, so you can see who is overdue and who is booked. Add a follow-up here with Add follow-up.

4.5 My Session Reports

Your own monthly numbers, for supervision or your records.

4.6 Your Settings

Your daily routine

  • Open Schedule and read today’s list.
  • Before each session, glance at Case history.
  • After each session, click Complete session (notes, status, next follow-up).
  • At the end of the day, clear the number on Session Notes.

Part 5 · For clinic ownersClinic owner guide

You can do everything the receptionist and therapist can. This part covers what only you set up: services, staff, access, settings and your plan.

5.1 Overview and Analytics

For month-end, use Monthly Report (shown in Part 3) — it covers the whole clinic.

5.2 Services — what you offer and what it costs

  1. Click Add Service (or the pencil on an existing card).
  2. Give it a name, a short description, a category (Counselling, Therapy, Assessment, Career or Other), the duration and an icon. The duration is the same for in-person and online — only the price differs.
  3. Click Save Service.
  4. Then set the price per therapist in Staff → Schedule & Pricing (next section).
Good to know

The software starts with twelve ready-made services (for example Initial Consultation, Individual Therapy, Psychological Assessment) so you do not begin from nothing. Rename or switch off any you do not need.

5.3 Staff — people, prices and access

Add a person

  1. Click Add Staff. The New Staff Account window opens.
  2. Choose the Role: Receptionist for the front desk, Psychologist for a therapist, or Staff for other support people.
  3. Fill in the full name, job title, login email and a first password (at least 8 characters). A photo is optional.
  4. Under Dashboard access, tick what this person may open (see below), then save the account at the bottom of the window.
  5. Tell them their email and password privately, and ask them to keep it safe.

Set a therapist’s services, prices and hours

Click Schedule & Pricing on a therapist. There are two tabs:

  • Services & Pricing: for each service switch In-person and/or Online on and type the price. A service is bookable only when at least one is on.
  • Availability: the weekly hours when that therapist can be booked.

Control what each person can open

Support staff always get Appointments and Patients. Tick the extra boxes — Billing, Analytics, Settings & Services — only for people you trust with them. The change works the next time they open the page.

Give the least access needed

Most receptionists need Appointments, Patients and Billing only. Keep “Settings & Services” for yourself or a manager.

5.4 Settings and Subscription

Share your booking link

Put the booking link on your website, Instagram or WhatsApp. Clients can then book without a login.

Do not let the plan run out

When the trial or plan ends, the whole clinic is locked (see Part 8, “Everything is locked”). Pay a few days early and watch the Days remaining number.

Part 6The client’s side & the platform admin

6.1 The public booking page

Every clinic has its own link, for example your-site/book/your-clinic. Clients need no account. They go through five steps: Details → Practitioner → Session → Schedule → Confirm.

What staff see

The booking appears in Appointments. A new person also becomes a Lead and turns into a Patient once the first booking is completed.

6.2 The client tracking page

After booking, the client gets a private tracking link by message or email. It shows the status of the booking and the details. It needs no login. Keep the link private — anyone with it can see the booking.

Part 7Platform admin

This is for the person who owns the whole platform and looks after many clinics. It uses a different login page, /admin. Clinic staff never need it.

Still stuck?

Write down: who you are (role), what you clicked, what you expected, and what happened — plus a screenshot. That lets the owner or IT person fix it in minutes.

Part 9Good habits & checklists

9.1 Protecting client privacy

  • Client records and session notes are private health information. Open them only for your work.
  • Use your own login always. Never use someone else’s.
  • Log out when you leave a shared computer, and lock your screen when you step away.
  • Do not send a client’s tracking link to anyone but the client.
  • Owners: when someone leaves the clinic, disable their login on the same day (Staff → red icon).
  • Give each person only the access they need.

9.2 Daily checklists

ReceptionistOwner
  • Open Follow-ups: book the overdue ones first.
  • Check Appointments for Pending / Verifying Payment.
  • Return calls and clear Leads.
  • Through the day: New Booking for each enquiry.
  • End of day: clear Pending Payments for today.
  • Glance at Overview and the bell.
  • Weekly: check Follow-up adherence in the Monthly Report.
  • Monthly: print or download the Monthly Report.
  • Keep an eye on Subscription → Days remaining.
  • Review who has which Dashboard access.
